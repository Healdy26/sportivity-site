Somebody in your business is quietly failing right now, and they do not know it.

You know. You have known since about June. You have mentioned it to one other person, carefully, in the way you do when you would rather it was not repeated. And every time you have thought about saying something to them directly, you have found a reason not to.

The bill for that turns up later. It always does.

## I was that manager

I ran a business for years and I was, without question, the softest touch in the building.

I told myself it was kindness. I was warm, I was approachable, people liked working for me, and I was proud of it. What I actually was, was a people pleaser. I wanted everybody to leave the room feeling good about me. That is not the same as leading them, and it took me a breakdown to work out the difference.

Here is what people pleasing looks like from the inside. You never decide to avoid the conversation. You just keep finding that today is a bad day for it. Their mum is not well. It is nearly Christmas. There is a big job on and you cannot afford the atmosphere in the office. Next month will be quieter.

Every one of those reasons is genuine. And every one of them is a reason.

Meanwhile the person carries on, not knowing, doing the thing you decided not to mention.

Who is that kind to?

## The date that made me write this

Two things shifted in employment law this autumn. I am not going to give you the compliance version, because your inbox is already doing that for you.

From 1 October, the time limit for bringing most employment tribunal claims went from three months to six. It doubled.

From 30 October, employers have to take "all reasonable steps" to prevent harassment at work, and become liable where a third party does it to one of your people. A customer. A contractor.

Francesca Wild, a partner at Morr and Co, made the point that actually matters. There is now a much longer stretch of uncertainty once somebody has left or been dismissed.

Sit with that for a second. The law has not changed how likely you are to get it wrong. It has doubled how long you carry it.

And a claim almost never starts at the dismissal. It starts months earlier, at the conversation nobody had.

## Why good people swerve it

I do not think it is cowardice, and I would argue with anyone who says it is.

You are frightened of making it worse. You have watched a clumsy conversation turn a decent employee into a resentful one, and you would rather keep the problem you have got than swap it for that one.

You are also not sure it is fair, because you have written nothing down and you cannot honestly remember what happened in June.

And underneath both of those sits the bit nobody says out loud. If you name it, you then have to do something about it. Doing something is work.

So you wait. And waiting feels like a decision you have not made yet, when really it is the decision.

## What to do on Monday

You already know the name. You thought of them in the first paragraph.

Go and have the conversation, and use a shape so you do not waffle your way round it. This is adult to adult, straight out of transactional analysis, and it is four sentences.

What I noticed. Specific and factual. Not "your attitude". The thing you saw, on the day you saw it.

What the effect was. On the work, and on the people picking it up. Not on how you feel about them.

What I need instead. Plainly. Do not make them guess and then mark them down for guessing wrong.

What help looks like, and when we will look at it again. Put a date on it. A real one, in the diary.

Twenty minutes. No form, no HR software, no policy document.

That last bit matters more than it looks. "All reasonable steps" is not a handbook. You cannot meet it with a PDF nobody has opened. Reasonable steps means somebody actually noticing, and saying so, early. A team where nobody says the awkward thing is a team where nobody reports the bad thing either. Same silence, different invoice.

## The kindest thing I never did

The kindest thing I never did was leave good people alone with something they could have sorted in a fortnight.

I thought I was protecting them. I was protecting me, from twenty uncomfortable minutes, and I charged them the difference.

So. Who is it?

Go and tell them this week. Not because of a date in October. Because while it is still small, they can still fix it.
