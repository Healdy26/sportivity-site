A lad I was talking to recently told me he is about eighteen months off going out on his own.

He is good. Genuinely good at the thing he does, better than the person two rungs above him, and he knows it. That is most of the reason he wants out. He had the whole plan ready. The name, a website, the rough day rate he would charge, what he needs to earn in month one to stand still.

I asked him who chases the invoice when it does not get paid.

He laughed. Then he stopped laughing, because he realised he had not given it a single second of thought.

## The number that should make you pause

Santander published research on 1 October after asking a thousand UK business owners how it had actually gone. Thirty five per cent said they never planned to run a business at all. Thirty nine per cent did not understand the basics when they started. Seventy nine per cent learned almost everything on the job. Sixty per cent found it harder than they expected.

That last one usually gets read as grit. People quote it admiringly, like a war story.

I read it as a handover that never happened.

## You have only ever done job one

Here is the bit nobody draws out for you.

When you work for somebody else, you do one job. You do the work, and you get judged on the work. Somebody above you absorbs everything else, and because they absorb it quietly, you never once see it happening.

The minute you go on your own, four jobs turn up. And they turn up in this order.

First you are the person who does the work. You are already brilliant at that, which is the trap, because it feels like the business is going well when really only a quarter of it is.

Then you are the person who sells it. Not networking. Selling. Sitting opposite someone, saying a number out loud, and not flinching after you say it.

Then you are the person who collects the money. Which sounds like admin right up until a client who likes you very much simply does not pay you for eleven weeks.

Then, the one that breaks people, you are the person who manages other humans doing the work you used to do yourself. Badly, at first, while you stand there watching and say nothing.

Michael Gerber wrote about this decades ago in The E-Myth and the point still stands up. Most small businesses are not started by business people. They are started by brilliant technicians who assumed the craft was the job.

## Learn it on their money, not yours

Everybody online will tell you to jump. Back yourself. Take the leap.

I will tell you something less exciting and a lot more useful. Learn jobs two, three and four while you are still on somebody's payroll, because right now they are carrying the risk and you are getting paid to practise.

That is not playing it safe. It is the cheapest training you will ever get offered.

Think about what the same mistake costs in each place. Price a job wrong where you are now and your boss has a slightly worse quarter. Price a job wrong in year one of your own thing and you cannot pay your mortgage. Identical lesson. Wildly different invoice.

So here is what to do on Monday.

Find one thing in that building with money or people on the end of it, and ask to own it from start to finish. Not help with. Own. The renewal nobody wants to handle. The supplier who keeps letting everyone down. The new starter nobody has time to bring on properly.

Then, before you touch it, write down what good looks like. One side of paper. What will be true in twelve weeks if I have done this properly?

That last part matters more than the task does. Most people never write it down, so they finish the job with no real idea whether they were any good at it, and they carry that fog out of the door with them when they finally leave. You cannot improve, hand over, or honestly judge a thing you have never defined.

I did not know most of this when I started. I learned it the way those seventy nine per cent did, on the job, in front of people who were paying me. It works. ## And the thing about ambition is this

Wanting out is not the same as being ready to go. They are two different conversations, and the second one is the one worth having.

So, honestly. If the whole lot landed on you on Monday morning, the work, the selling, the chasing, the people, which of those four catches you out first?

Go and own that one this year. While somebody else is still paying for your mistakes.
