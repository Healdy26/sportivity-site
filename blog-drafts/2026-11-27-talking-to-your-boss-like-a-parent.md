You knock on a door that is already open.

You do it every week. You stand in the doorway with a piece of work you know is right, and you say, "Sorry, have you got two minutes? I just wanted to check it's OK if I send this."

It is OK. You knew it was OK before you walked over. Your boss glances at it, says "yeah, fine", and goes back to the screen.

And you walk away thinking you have been thorough. What you have actually done is tell the person who decides your next pay rise, again, that you need somebody to sign off your thinking.

## Three voices, and you only need one of them

In 1964 a psychiatrist called Eric Berne wrote a book called Games People Play. It sold millions, which is unusual for a book about psychiatry, and the reason is that the main idea is dead simple.

Berne said that whenever we speak, we speak from one of three places. He called them Parent, Adult and Child.

Parent is the voice you borrowed from whoever raised you. It tells people off or it looks after them. "You should have told me sooner." "Leave it with me, I'll sort it."

Child is the voice you had when you were small. It asks permission, says sorry, sulks, or tries to please. "Is it all right if I...?" "Sorry to bother you." "It's not fair, nobody tells me anything."

Adult is the one in the middle. It deals with what is in front of it. "Here is what I found, here is what I am going to do, shout if you see a problem."

None of it has anything to do with your age. You can hear the Child in a sixty year old director and the Adult in an apprentice.

## Why it sticks

Here is the part that matters for you.

Berne noticed that the voice you use invites a particular voice back. Speak as a Child and you pull the Parent out of the other person, whether they meant to bring it or not. "Is it OK if I send this?" can only really be answered with permission. So your boss gives permission. And giving permission is a parent's job.

Do that every week for two years and you have trained the pair of you. They check your work because you keep handing it over to be checked. You hand it over because they keep checking. Nobody chose it. And when a bigger job comes up, they try to picture you in it, and what they see is the person in the doorway.

I know this voice from the inside. I was a people pleaser for years, and the Child voice is the people pleaser's first language. It feels like good manners. A lot of the time it is fear in a nice jumper.

So is the answer to stop being polite? No. And it is not to start telling people what to do either. That is just swapping Child for Parent, and nobody promotes the person who lectures the room.

## Change one sentence

Adult to adult is what you are after. Two people looking at the same problem, level.

You don't need a course for it. You need to catch one sentence a day and say it again in your head before it leaves your mouth.

"Is it OK if I send this to the client?" becomes "I'm sending this to the client at two. Have a look before then if you want to."

"Sorry, I know you're busy, I just wondered what you wanted me to do about the supplier" becomes "The supplier has missed two deliveries. I think we give them one more and then switch. Are you happy with that?"

"Nobody told me the deadline had moved" becomes "I missed that the deadline moved. What's the best way for me to hear about changes like that?"

Look at what changed. You are still polite. Your boss can still say no. But you have turned up with a view, and you have given them something to agree with instead of something to approve. Those are different jobs, and only one of them makes you look like a peer.

It will feel rude the first few times. It isn't. Most managers are quietly relieved, because being everybody's parent is exhausting and they get enough of that at home.

Some won't be. Now and then you will meet a boss who needs you to stay small, and the Adult voice will get you told off. That is worth knowing as well, and better to find it out this month than in five years.

## This week

Pick the one conversation you have most often with the person above you. Write down what you normally say, word for word. Then write the Adult version underneath it, and use that one next time.

That is the whole task. One sentence.
