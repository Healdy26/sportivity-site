TITLE: The Two Years I Spent Waiting to Be Picked
SUBTITLE: I did the bigger job for two years and waited for someone to notice. Nobody did. Here is what I got wrong, and why it matters more now that vacancies have dropped and the only way up is inside the building.

I can still remember sitting in the car park afterwards, engine off, not going anywhere.

They had given the role to someone else. Someone who, as far as I could tell, had done less of the actual work than I had. I had been covering half that job for the best part of two years. Picking up the messes nobody wanted, smoothing things over with the difficult partner, staying late to fix the thing that would have blown up on Monday.

And I sat there thinking the thought that every ambitious person thinks at some point. They must not have seen it.

They hadn't. That was the honest answer. And it took me an embarrassingly long time to work out whose fault that was.

## Quiet competence is not a strategy

Here is what I believed back then, and I meet a version of me in almost every organisation I work in now.

I believed that if I did good work, consistently, without fuss, someone above me would eventually add it up and reward it. That the ledger was being kept. That there was a grown-up somewhere with a spreadsheet who knew exactly how much I was carrying.

There is no ledger. There is a busy manager with their own targets, their own boss, and about four minutes a week to think about your career.

I was not being overlooked because the work was poor. I was being overlooked because the work was invisible. I had built a role out of firefighting, and firefighting has a cruel quality to it. When you do it well, nothing happens. And nothing does not show up in a review.

## The market has quietly changed the rules

This matters more now than it did then.

UK vacancies sat at 707,000 in the March to May period this year, down again on the quarter and well below where they were before the pandemic. The escalator out of your job has slowed right down. At the same time, internal moves have gone the other way. Internal mobility has climbed from around half of all roles filled a few years ago to roughly six in ten.

Read those two numbers together and you get the situation Dan is actually in. Fewer doors out. More of the good jobs being handed to someone already in the building.

Which means the person who wins is not the best worker. It is the best worker whose name comes up in a room they are not sat in.

That is not politics. It is not sucking up. It is just how decisions get made when a manager has four minutes and a shortlist in their head.

## Do the job before you have the title, but do it where people can see

Herminia Ibarra, who studies how people actually make career transitions, makes a point I wish I had heard at 28. You do not think your way into a new role. You act your way into it. The identity follows the doing, not the other way round.

I was already halfway there. I was doing the job. I just did it in a way that produced no evidence.

So here is the thing I would go back and tell myself, and it is the one thing I would ask you to do this month.

Pick one problem that your boss's boss genuinely loses sleep over. Not your problem. Theirs. The one that comes up in every leadership meeting and never gets solved because everyone is too busy.

Take a piece of it. A piece, not the whole thing. Solve that piece properly.

Then, and this is the bit I could not do for two years, write it down and send it. Half a page. Here was the problem, here is what I did, here is what it saved us, here is what I would do next with a bit more room.

That's it. No performance, no self-promotion cringe. Just evidence, in writing, in the hands of the person who decides.

## The bit nobody tells you

I never sent that half page. I left instead, and eventually built my own thing, and I do not regret where it took me. But I would be lying if I said the leaving was some brave strategic move. It was mostly frustration with a decision I never actually asked anyone to make.

That is the part that still bothers me. Not that I did not get the job. That I never once said out loud that I wanted it.

If you are the one carrying more than your title says, and you are waiting for someone to notice, they are not going to. Not because they do not rate you. Because they are not looking.

So what is the one thing you are doing brilliantly this week that nobody above you will ever hear about?

Go and tell them.
