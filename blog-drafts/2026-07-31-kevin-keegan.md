Some people lead with a spreadsheet. Kevin Keegan led with his heart hanging out of his chest for the whole country to see. This week, at 75, he passed away after a battle with cancer, and I found myself thinking about him more than I expected to.

Because whatever you know about football, you know that one moment. A manager, live on television, jabbing his finger and half-breaking as he said, "I would love it if we beat them. Love it." That was Keegan in 1996, in charge of Newcastle, chasing the title, cracking under the mind games of Alex Ferguson's Manchester United down the road. It's one of the most human things ever shown on a sports channel, and nearly thirty years on we all still remember it.

## The player, and the man

Before any of that, he was one of the best in the world. Two Ballon d'Ors. A European Cup with Liverpool. Captain of England. He wasn't the biggest or the most gifted athlete on the pitch, and he'd tell you that himself. He got there on graft, drive, and a kind of relentless want that you couldn't teach if you tried.

But it's not the medals I'll remember him for. It's the passion that came off him in waves. Players ran through walls for Keegan because you could see, plainly, that he cared. There was nothing hidden, nothing corporate, no mask. What you saw was what he felt, and people follow that. They always have.

## What his kind of leadership gives you

I've met a few leaders like him, and worked for one or two. The heart-on-the-sleeve type. You know exactly where you stand with them. They light a room up. They make you believe you can do more than you thought, because their belief is so obviously real. That's a rare gift, and in a world of guarded, careful, say-nothing leadership, it's worth more than ever.

Passion is contagious. A leader who genuinely cares, out loud, pulls better work out of people than any incentive scheme ever printed. Keegan had that in bucketloads, and it's why grown adults are grieving a man most of them never met.

## And what it costs

Here's the honest bit, said with nothing but respect. The same open heart that made Keegan magic also left him exposed. That famous moment wasn't triumph, it was a man cracking under pressure with the cameras rolling. His Newcastle side played some of the most thrilling football England has ever seen, and they didn't win the league. The heart that made them soar also, in the end, ran the show a little too much.

That's not a criticism of him. It's the truth about his kind of leadership, and mine, and maybe yours. Leading with your heart is a superpower and a risk in the same breath. The trick, and it's the work of a lifetime, is to keep the fire and not let it burn the house down. To care that much and still hold your nerve when the moment gets big.

Keegan gave the game its heart. He just showed us, too, that heart on its own isn't quite enough. You need the steel to go with it. Funnily enough, that's the exact thing I found myself writing about our new Prime Minister only last week.

Rest easy, Kevin. Thanks for the passion, and for the reminder. Thoughts are with his wife and daughters and everyone who loved him.
