Fifty-five minutes into a World Cup semi-final, England were winning. Anthony Gordon had put them one up against Argentina, they were the better side, and a first final in a generation was there for the taking.

Then something shifted. Thomas Tuchel pulled his shape back into a 5-4-1, dropped deep, and set the team up to protect the one goal rather than go and get a second. You could feel it through the screen. England stopped playing to win and started playing not to lose.

You know how it ended. Argentina, driven by a still-brilliant Messi, poured forward, got their equaliser through Enzo Fernández, and then broke England's hearts through Lautaro Martínez in the last minute of stoppage time. Two-one. Out.

## Playing not to lose is how you lose

I'm not here to kick a manager while he's down. Hindsight is a wonderful thing and I've never coached at that level. But the pattern is one I've seen a hundred times, in sport and in business, and it's worth naming.

The second you decide to protect a lead instead of extend it, you hand the initiative to the other lot. You invite the pressure. You spend the rest of the game hanging on, and hanging on is exhausting and it rarely holds. Sides that sit on a one-goal lead concede it far more often than sides that go and kill the game off.

It looks sensible in the moment. Safe feels responsible. But safe isn't neutral. Safe is a decision, and often it's the riskiest one on the table.

## I've done it too

Here's my confession. I've done exactly this in business, more than once. You have a good year, you build something up, and quietly your mindset flips from "how do we grow this" to "how do we not lose this". You stop taking the bold call. You protect the client rather than chase the better one. You defend the patch instead of expanding it.

And it feels grown-up. It feels like stewardship. But nine times out of ten, the business that's playing not to lose gets caught by the one that's still playing to win. Standing still isn't standing still. In a moving game, it's going backwards.

The hardest thing as a leader isn't finding courage when you're behind. Anyone can throw the kitchen sink at it when they've nothing to lose. The hard thing is keeping your nerve when you're **ahead**, when you've got something worth protecting and every instinct is screaming at you to sit on it.

## The question to ask yourself

So here's the one I'd leave you with, and it's the one I have to ask myself. Where in your business are you currently playing not to lose?

Where have you gone from building to defending without really deciding to? The client you're keeping happy instead of the market you should be chasing. The safe hire instead of the brave one. The lead you're sitting on instead of the game you should be killing off.

Going ahead is the easy part. Staying brave once you're there is the whole job. England forgot that for thirty-five minutes, and it cost them a World Cup final. It tends to cost the rest of us a good deal more, a lot more slowly, and we barely notice it happening.
