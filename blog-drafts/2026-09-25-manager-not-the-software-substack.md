TITLE: Same Software. Eight Times the Result.
SUBTITLE: You bought the licences, ran the webinar, and six months later nothing had changed. Gallup found the one variable that decides whether AI does anything at all in your business, and it is not the tool.

A director I know bought the whole company AI licences last autumn. Everybody got one. There was a launch email, a lunchtime webinar with a slide deck, and a line in the board pack about being ahead of the curve.

I asked him in the spring how it was going.

He did that face. The one where somebody is about to tell you the truth and is slightly annoyed about it. "Honestly? Two people use it properly. The rest opened it once."

He had spent thousands. He was now fairly sure AI was overhyped.

It was not overhyped. He had just bought the wrong thing.

## The only AI statistic I would put in front of a business owner

Gallup ran a piece of work earlier this year that I keep coming back to. They asked 102 HR directors at Fortune 500 companies about AI. Ninety nine per cent said it mattered to their strategy. Half of them then said they were not confident their own managers could guide anybody on how to use it.

Read that again, because these are the big lads. Budgets. Learning and development teams. Actual AI centres of excellence. Half of them do not trust the layer of people who have to make it real.

But the number that stopped me was on the employee side, from a survey of over 23,000 workers.

Where people strongly agreed that their manager championed AI, 33 per cent said it had transformed how the work gets done.

Where they did not, 4 per cent.

Same software. Same licence fee. Same webinar. Eight times the result. The only thing that changed was the person standing in front of the team.

## It was never a tools problem

Here is the trap, and it is such an easy one to fall into when you are running a business and you can feel the world moving.

Buying is doing. Buying feels like progress. You can put a purchase order through on a Tuesday and tell yourself the AI question is handled. Nobody can put "changed how forty people actually work" through on a Tuesday.

So we keep buying the tool and skipping the bit that makes tools work, which is a human being who uses it in front of other human beings and makes it safe to be rubbish at it for a fortnight.

That is what "champions it" means in the Gallup data. Not enthusiasm. Not a poster. It means somebody senior enough to matter is visibly doing it badly in public, sharing what worked, and not quietly judging the person who is scared of it.

The Chartered Management Institute found around seven in ten UK managers are already using generative AI for advice and guidance at work. So most of them are using it. They are just using it in private, on their own, like a bit of a secret. None of that leaks out to the team unless somebody makes it leak.

## Three things that cost you nothing

If you have bought licences and nothing has happened, do not buy more. Do this instead.

**Pick one task and do it out loud.** Not "use AI more". One real job. The monthly report, the tender summary, the first draft of the client email. Have one manager do that task with it, in a meeting, on the screen, including the bit where the first attempt comes back as waffle and they have to ask again properly.

**Make the failures the thing you share, not the wins.** Every team has one person who got a rubbish answer, decided the whole thing was nonsense, and never opened it again. They are not being difficult. Nobody showed them that the first answer is always the worst one. Ask in your next team meeting what people have tried that did not work. That single question does more than any webinar.

**Ask your managers the honest question.** Not "are you using it". Ask: could you sit with someone in your team for twenty minutes and show them how to use it for their actual job. If the answer is no, that is your gap, and it is a leadership gap wearing a software costume.

## The bit nobody says out loud

We promote people because they are good at the job. Then we hand them a team and train them in more or less nothing. Then we ask them to lead everyone through the biggest change to work in a generation.

And now the routes that used to fix that are thinning. Team Leader Level 3 and Operations Manager Level 5 lost their funding for new starts this month. So the manager who was never developed is not about to be developed by accident either.

If the Fortune 500 with a centre of excellence cannot get this to work, the licences were never the answer for the rest of us either.

The good news, if you want it, is that the fix is not expensive. It is somebody being willing to look daft in front of their team for a couple of weeks.
