Every time I ask an organisation to show me how they do something well, I get sent the finished thing.

The polished document. The framework with the nice diagram. The bit they are proud of.

And it is almost always useless to me.

Not because it is bad. Because all the working out has been rubbed off it. You cannot see a single decision in there. You cannot see what they tried first, or what they argued about, or the week it did not work and they nearly binned it.

I have been gathering examples of good practice for a piece of work, and the most useful conversations by a mile have been with people who are halfway through changing something. Mid-review. Bits of it broken. Genuinely not sure yet whether the new version is any better.

That is where the learning sits. The messy middle, happening live.

The finished version tells you where somebody ended up. The messy middle tells you how they got there, and that is the only part you can actually copy.

So if you run a team, this changes the question you ask in the room.

Not "what's going well". You will get a list of wins, everyone will nod, and nobody will learn a thing.

Ask "what are you in the middle of changing, and what have you not cracked yet?"

It feels riskier. It is not. It is the difference between a room full of people reporting and a room full of people thinking, and everybody sat in there knows exactly which one it is.

When did you last let somebody see your working out instead of your finished version?
