You are the name on the email. The buttons are in somebody else's building.

I keep running into this one, and it took me a while to see it as a pattern rather than just a bad week.

Somebody gets handed a standard to enforce. They did not write it. They cannot change it. And the thing people actually need in order to meet it, the training, the booking, the sign-off, sits with an organisation they have no authority over at all.

So the emails come to them. The same three questions, over and over. They cannot answer any of them, because the answers belong somewhere else. They pass them on and say "sorry, that one isn't ours". Which is true, and which makes them sound like they are hiding behind something.

Then everybody gets cross with the person holding the clipboard.

If that is you, hear this. You are not bad at your job. You are in a trap with a shape to it, and being nicer in your replies will not get you out.

What got me out was writing one sentence down and reading it back.

We set the requirement, we carry the frustration, and we hold none of the levers.

Once that is on paper it stops being a mood and becomes a problem with an owner. So instead of answering the next one individually, you go to whoever holds the levers with the count. This many emails. These are the faults. Here are the fixes, and here is the one that is yours.

That conversation is awkward. It is also far cheaper than another month of forwarding.

And the honest part is this. The trap was never that people were annoyed with me. It was that absorbing it felt like good service. It isn't. It is a polite way of letting a broken route stay broken, and I was the one keeping it alive.

So, question. What are you apologising for every single week that you have never once asked the owner to fix?
