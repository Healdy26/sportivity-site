Somebody asked me last week to confirm exactly what capacity I was sitting in on a piece of work.

Fair question. I wear a few hats.

The easy reply was the one that made me look cleanest. Name the main hat, say nothing else, move on. Nobody would have chased it.

I listed the other one instead. The one that could, if you squinted at it, look like an interest in the outcome.

Not because I am noble. Because of something I learned the hard way about the things you hope nobody mentions.

They only have power while you are the only person who knows.

Say it yourself, first, and it stops being a secret and becomes a fact about you. Facts are manageable. Secrets are not, and they get more expensive every week you sit on one.

I have watched good people lose work over this. Not over the conflict itself. Over the client finding it on their own, months in, and then having to wonder what else was in there.

It works smaller than that too. The gap in your CV. The client you left on bad terms. The bit of the job you are not confident on yet. Say it first, plainly, with no apology bolted onto it, and watch how quickly it stops being a problem.

People do not trust you because there is nothing awkward about you. Nobody has nothing.

They trust you because you told them the awkward bit before they had to go looking for it.

What are you quietly hoping nobody asks you about this week?
