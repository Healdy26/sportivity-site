"Strongest in the batch."

I wrote that in a report once and somebody made me take it out. They were right, and it has changed how I look at appraisals ever since.

Read it back. It says nothing at all about the work. It only says something about who else happened to land on the pile that week.

Be in a strong batch and you are average. Be in a weak one and you are a star. Same work, same person, different week.

And that is exactly what happens to good people in appraisals.

You are not being measured against what the job needs. You are being measured against the others in the room, in the order they got looked at, by somebody trying to hold all of you in their head at once. That is how a solid performer ends up being called steady. It is also why the feedback never quite tells you what to do differently. There is nothing to do. The comparison was the judgement.

So the rule I work to now is this. Every one is judged on its own, against what the thing actually needs. Never against the others.

Two things follow, and the second one is the one people miss.

First, you have to decide what the job needs before you look at anybody.

Second, you cannot mark somebody down for missing something the job never asked for. Sounds obvious. It is the most common unfair judgement I see, and it is almost always sincere. Somebody has a picture in their head of what good looks like, the picture includes things the role does not actually require, and the gap between the two gets written up as a weakness in a real person's review.

If you appraise people, try it on the next one. Cover up the others. Judge that one against the job.

And if you are the one on the receiving end, and the feedback never quite lands, it might not be you. Ask it straight. Compared to what?
