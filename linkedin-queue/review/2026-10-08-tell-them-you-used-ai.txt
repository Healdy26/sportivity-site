I put a line in an email this week that I think more people should be putting in theirs.

I had written something up from a long conversation with somebody. A proper piece of work. Their words, their story, going out with their name attached to it.

So I told them how it got made.

The call was transcribed by an app. I used AI to help with the first draft. I have checked every word, but you are the judge of whether it is right. And if you would rather AI stayed out of it altogether, say so and I will write it up from the recording myself.

Then nothing goes anywhere until they have read it and said yes in writing.

I could have said none of that. Hardly anybody does. The work was good, the words were genuinely theirs, and they would almost certainly never have asked.

But here is what I have worked out about that choice.

Everybody is using it. Almost nobody is saying so. Which means the first time a client works out that your words came out of a machine, and you never mentioned it, the problem is not the machine at all. It is that they now have to wonder what else you have not mentioned.

Saying it costs you three lines and about four seconds of awkwardness. It is the cheapest trust you will ever buy, and it hands them a veto they will almost never use.

There is a selfish reason too. Once you have said it out loud, you check the work properly, because you have just invited somebody to go looking.

If you use AI on work that goes out with a client's name on it, and you do, when did you last tell one of them?
