The default is a decision. You just did not make it.

This is one of the quieter ways an organisation ends up being unfair, and I keep running into it.

There is a rule. The rule has a set consequence attached. Something goes wrong, the consequence gets applied, and everybody walks away feeling like no decision was taken. The policy decided. Nobody is to blame. It is just what happens.

Except the policy could not see what you can see.

It could not see that this was a first time. It could not see that the person told you before you found out, and had already tried everything else. It could not see that the same consequence will flatten somebody who is hanging on by their fingertips and barely register with somebody who can absorb it without noticing.

A policy cannot see any of that. It was never built to. That is what you are for.

And I am not arguing for going soft, or making it up as you go along. Consistency matters. People need to know where the line is, and a rule you bend for your mates is worse than having no rule at all. So write down why you landed where you landed. Then the next person in your chair can follow the reasoning, and it holds up when somebody asks you to explain it.

But let it be a decision.

The question I ask myself now, before anything like this goes out, is just this. Did I decide it, or did I let it happen?

Because letting it happen is still a choice. It is the one where nobody has to feel responsible. And it is always the one the person on the other end remembers.

When did you last apply the standard consequence without actually deciding anything?
