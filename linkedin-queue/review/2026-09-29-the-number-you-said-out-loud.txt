I gave someone a number on a call last week.

Said it quickly, from memory, the way you do when you know your own business inside out. Roughly this many, running at about this percentage.

Then I went and checked it.

The percentage was near enough spot on. The count was miles out. Not double, not a rounding thing. Just wrong, confidently, in a meeting, by me.

Nobody would have caught it. It would have gone into somebody's notes, then into a report, and six weeks later I would have been reading my own made-up figure back off a slide and nodding along at it.

So I corrected it in writing the next morning, before anyone used it.

Here is what I think is actually going on, and it is not carelessness.

When you know something well, numbers stop being numbers and turn into a feeling. You know it is going fine. Your brain reaches for a figure that matches the feeling and hands you one, and it sounds right to you because it matches how things are going.

The feeling was accurate. The number was fiction.

Senior people do this constantly, and it is one of the quieter ways bad decisions get made, because a number said with confidence by the boss does not get checked by anybody else in the room.

Two things I would do with that.

Say "I think, and I'll confirm it" when you are quoting from memory. It costs you nothing and it tells everyone else that checking is normal here.

Then when you get one wrong, go back and fix it before anybody builds on it. Not as a confession. Just the right number, sent early, no drama.

What number do you repeat about your business that you have not actually looked at this year?
