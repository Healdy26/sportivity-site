The fastest way to lose a customer's trust is to answer them quickly and be wrong.

I keep seeing the same thing when a business brings someone new in and points them at the inbox.

They are keen. They want to prove they were the right pick. So they answer everything, fast, and mostly they answer it well. Then a question comes in that turns on a rule, or on something the business does not actually control. And because they are keen, they say yes.

Now somebody senior has to go back and take that yes away.

That costs far more than a slow answer ever would have. The customer does not remember the eleven quick replies. They remember the one that got reversed.

So here is what I would teach on day one, before anything else.

Give them a holding line, and tell them out loud that using it is allowed.

"Good question. I'm checking that properly and you'll have an answer from me by Thursday."

Nine seconds of work. It buys the whole business time and it does not make anyone look daft. Being checked reads as careful. Being reversed reads as chaotic.

The bit most owners miss is the permission. If you have never said that holding is fine, a new person assumes not knowing is failure, and they guess. Every time. Not because they are reckless. Because they think a fast yes is what you hired them for.

Speed is the easiest thing to teach. Knowing when not to be fast is the hard bit, and nobody picks that up by osmosis.

Who in your business is answering questions today that they are not actually allowed to answer?
