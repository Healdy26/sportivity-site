A farmer's horse runs off. The neighbours come round. Terrible luck, they say.

He says, maybe so, maybe not.

A week later the horse comes back and brings a dozen wild ones with it. Neighbours again. What great luck.

Maybe so, maybe not.

His son tries to break one of them in, comes off it and breaks his leg. Terrible luck.

Maybe so, maybe not.

Then the army turns up recruiting every able bodied lad in the village, and walks straight past a boy who cannot walk.

You already know what he said.

I came across that story during a morning meditation a while back and it has stayed with me since, because I keep watching owners write a whole year off in September on the back of one bad month.

The contract that fell through in March is the reason there was room for the one that came in June. The person who left, the one it hurt to lose, had been quietly holding two others back for years. You cannot see any of that at the time. You only ever see it looking backwards.

I am not telling you to be relaxed about a bad month. Numbers matter and I am the last person who would tell you otherwise.

I am saying be careful how fast you decide what a thing means.

Because the label you stick on it in week one shapes every decision you make in week two.

What did you write off this year that you might not actually be finished with?
