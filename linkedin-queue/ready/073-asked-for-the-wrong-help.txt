A lad asked me recently if I would coach him. He had been passed over for the second time and he wanted to fix whatever was wrong with him.

I asked him what he thought was wrong.

He did not know. That was the actual problem. He had bought a book, done a course on influencing, and he was lining up a coach, because that is the order you are told to do it in.

So I asked him something else. Who in that building has the job you want, and have you ever sat down with them for half an hour?

Quiet. Then: "I wouldn't know what to ask."

Here is a distinction that took me far too long to get straight, and it is worth having clear before you spend any money.

A coach does not need to have done your job. That is rather the point of them. They ask the question you have been walking round for months, then they sit in the silence while you squirm, and the answer you come out with is yours. I had a mentor years ago who left my head aching. I would walk out with no answers at all and a much better set of questions.

Somebody who has actually walked the path is a different animal. They know the loose rocks and the bend where everybody slips. They will tell you the thing that is nowhere in the job description and that nobody will say to your face.

He did not need help finding his own answers. He needed ten minutes with somebody who had sat in the chair he was after.

Two different jobs. We ask for the wrong one all the time, usually because the wrong one is easier to buy. A course has a price and a date on it. A difficult conversation has neither.

So which is it for you at the minute?

If you cannot name the obstacle, get coached.

If you can name it exactly and you have just never seen it done, go and find somebody who has done it.

Either way it starts with one conversation you have been putting off. Who is that with?
