The best plumber in the firm is now running the plumbers.

He was brilliant at the job. That is exactly why you promoted him. Everybody nodded, it was the obvious call, and he was chuffed to bits.

So what did he get on his first morning as a manager?

A congratulations email and a new job title.

That is the whole handover.

Most managers in this country got about that much. Promoted because they were good at the work, trained in next to nothing about people, handed a team, and left to figure it out on an evening.

And they do figure some of it out. They crack the parts that feel like doing the job, because that bit is familiar. Rotas. Chasing. Quality. Putting fires out.

What they do not crack is the human half, because nobody ever showed them, and they are certainly not going to put their hand up and say they are struggling. Not when they were promoted for being the one who knows what he is doing.

So they manage the way they were managed. Which is how a bad habit survives thirty years in a trade.

Then a young lad starts, it goes badly, and we decide the young lad is the problem.

Your best doer did not get worse the day you promoted him. You changed his job completely and told him nothing about the new one.

You do not need a course to start sorting this. You need one conversation.

Sit down with whoever you promoted this year. Ask them this, then stay quiet long enough to get a real answer.

Which part of managing people are you quietly winging?

Whatever comes back is the thing to help them with. And if they tell you nothing at all, that is your answer as well.

So who did you promote this year, and what did you genuinely give them?
