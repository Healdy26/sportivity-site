You took people on, and you ended up poorer and busier than when you were doing it all yourself.

If that is you, I want to say one thing plainly. That is not a cost problem.

Everyone treats it as one. The wage bill, the late payers, the price of absolutely everything. All of it real, and I am not going to pretend it is not.

But I have sat with enough owners now to know what is usually going on underneath, and it is rarely the numbers.

You did not build a business. You built yourself a harder job with more liability attached to it.

Here is how it happens, and nobody does it on purpose.

You are good at the work. So you take people on to do more of the work. But you keep the quoting. You keep the awkward client. You keep the bits that need doing properly, because you are quickest and you care most.

You handed over the tasks and kept every single decision.

So now there are more people, more wages and more worry, and there is still only one person in the building who can decide anything.

That is not a business. That is you with witnesses.

The fix is not another ten hours a week. You have not got another ten hours, and if you had them you would have spent them by Wednesday.

Try this instead, and it takes twenty minutes.

Write down every task you did last week. Then mark only the ones that genuinely needed you. Not the ones you are fastest at. Not the ones you enjoy. The ones nobody else in that building could have done.

Count what is left. That number is your actual job.

Everything else is a handover you have been putting off, and it is costing you more than the wages are.

So be honest with yourself. If you walked away for a fortnight, would it grow, or would it stop?
