The busiest spell of my working life rolled straight into a month where the phone did not ring.

Interviews, tenders, pitches. Roles I wanted and work I would have loved. A fair few of them did not land.

And my head had plenty to say about that.

You know the voice. Maybe you are not good enough. Maybe the last eight years were luck. Maybe that was the top and it is downhill from here.

That voice does not knock politely. It walks in and sits down.

Here is what I have learned to do with it, and it is the most useful thing I can give you today.

I do not argue with it any more. You cannot win that argument. It has already heard all your evidence and it is not interested.

I answer it with a practice instead.

Name what is genuinely good right now, and say it out loud. Not a list in your head on the way to the car. Out loud, where you can hear it.

Sounds soft. It is not. The catastrophe in your head needs you to keep it vague, and it cannot survive contact with anything specific and true.

Because here is the thing about peaks and troughs. The work goes up and it goes down. It always has and it always will. The only part you actually control is the mindset sat underneath both of them.

The peak is not proof you have made it.

The trough is not proof you are finished.

It is weather. And you are not the weather.

So if you are in a quiet patch right now, quietly wondering whether you have lost it, you have not. You are between things. That is not the same as finished.

What is good in your week that you have not said out loud to anybody yet?
