I have got a master's in business and a level seven in leadership.

I am glad I did them. They opened doors. They got me sat in rooms I would not otherwise have been in.

But I have never once been given a piece of work because of either of them.

The paper gets you the interview. The human gets the job. And then the human keeps the relationship going for the next ten years, which is where the actual work comes from.

I say this because there are two people who both need to hear it.

The first is the lad who thinks he is one qualification away. He is not. He is one honest conversation away, with somebody who already rates him, and he keeps putting that conversation off by signing up for another course instead. Studying is a very respectable way of hiding.

The second is the owner sat with a stack of CVs, sorting them by the top third of the page. Degree, no degree, degree. I understand why. It is quick and it feels defensible.

But I have worked with enough people now to know that the thing which predicts whether they will be any good is not on that part of the page. It is whether they have ever had to sort something out themselves when nobody was coming to help.

So ask about that instead. Tell me about a time you were out of your depth and could not hand it on to anyone. What did you do first?

You will learn more in those four minutes than in their whole education section.

What have you actually been hired for? The certificate, or the way you are with people?
