I went camping with my kids and barely said a word for the first day.

Not sulking. Just tired, and there was nothing that needed saying.

And because I was not talking, everybody else did. My dad in the tent. A mate I had not seen for a year, who got about four minutes into a conversation about nothing much before telling me something that was clearly sat heavy on him.

I did nothing to make that happen. I left a gap and I did not fill it.

That stayed with me, because filling gaps is pretty much the job description of every leader I meet. You ask a question, you get three seconds of nothing, and the silence starts to feel like your fault. So you rescue it. You rephrase it, you offer a couple of options, you end up answering your own question.

And you never find out what they were about to say.

The honest answer to a hard question hardly ever arrives in the first three seconds. It turns up around eight, once the person has decided it is safe to give you the real version instead of the tidy one.

So in your next one to one, try this and nothing else.

Ask one question. Then count to ten in your head before you make a sound. It will feel ridiculous for about four of those seconds. Do it anyway.

If they fill the silence, you have got your answer. If they do not, ask what else, and count again.

You do not need a cleverer question. You need to get comfortable in the gap after it.

When did you last stop talking long enough to find out what somebody actually thinks?
