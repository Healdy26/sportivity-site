First week with a new group of students, and I had the list ready.

No phones. No food. No hoods up.

I binned it before I said a word of it, and asked them a question instead.

What kind of culture do we want in this room?

They built it themselves. Took a lot longer than reading my list out would have. And every single thing on it got kept, because it was theirs and not mine.

I have thought about that every time I have picked up a staff handbook since.

Because most of the rules in most businesses are a leader's anxiety, written down. Somebody did something once, it annoyed you, and the response was a clause. Do that for five years and you have got a document nobody reads, enforced by nobody, about behaviour that stopped happening ages ago.

Rules tell people what you are frightened of. Agreements tell people what you are for.

And a rule only works while somebody is watching. That is the whole trouble with it.

Try this with your team. You will need about forty minutes and the humility to shut up for most of them.

Ask what we want this place to feel like on a good day. Then ask what gets in the way of that. Then ask what we are going to do about it, and write down what they say in their words, not yours.

Stick it somewhere people actually see it.

Then the first time it gets broken, and it will, you are not the bloke enforcing a rule. You are the one pointing at something the room agreed to.

What is in your handbook that is really just something that annoyed you once?
