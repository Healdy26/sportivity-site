A director told me AI was overhyped rubbish.

I asked him to show me. He opened it up, typed "write me an email to a client", and turned the screen round with a look that said, see.

And fair enough. What came back was grey waffle that could have been about anyone selling anything to anybody.

So I asked him something.

If a brand new employee walked in on their first morning and you said those exact seven words to them, what would you get back?

He laughed. Because he knew.

The same grey waffle. Not because the new starter is thick. Because you have told them nothing. No client name. No history. No point to the email. No sense of how you talk.

You have handed a capable person a blank page and expected them to read your mind.

Most leaders are not bad at AI. They are bad at briefing.

So give it what you would give a sharp new starter on a good day. Who you are and who it is for. What the job actually needs to do, not "write an email" but "get her to book a review without making her feel guilty about the gap". An example of one you were proud of, because it will copy that far better than any adjective you throw at it. And the guardrails. Under 120 words, no jargon, do not mention the missed appointments.

Same tool. Completely different answer.

Here is the bit that stopped me in my tracks though.

If your brief to the machine is one vague line and a hope, what do your briefs to your team look like?

The blank page you keep handing the AI might be the same one landing on their desks.

Next time something comes back half baked, from a person or a tool, be honest with yourself. Could they not do it? Or did you never really say what you wanted?
