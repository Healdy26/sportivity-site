Somebody told me early on that I should soften the way I talk.

Not in those words. It was dressed up nicer than that. Something about how the accent might not travel in certain rooms, and whether I had thought about how I came across at that level.

I was ambitious, so I took it as development. And for about a year I did it. Flattened the vowels out. Picked longer words than I needed. Said "in terms of" quite a lot, which is a thing I still catch myself doing when I am nervous.

I was worse. Properly worse.

Not because the accent was the magic. Because all the energy that should have been on the point was going on the performance. I was listening to myself talk instead of watching the room.

It took me years to work out why that matters so much. People do not decide whether to trust you on the words. They decide on whether the words match the person saying them. The second those two come apart, something in the room goes quiet, and nobody can tell you afterwards what it was. They just did not buy it.

I know a bloke who changed his accent properly. Talks about leadership and being yourself for a living. I do not think he can hear it any more, and that is the sad bit.

So if somebody has told you to polish up, here is what I would do with it.

Record yourself explaining the thing to a mate. Just talking, no slides. Then record your presentation voice doing the same bit.

Play both back. One of them is you.

Work on the clarity. Work on the structure. Cut the waffle, because there is always waffle, and mine was "in terms of".

Leave the voice alone.

What have you been editing out of yourself that was never the problem?
