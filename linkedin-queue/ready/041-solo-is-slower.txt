I had a mentor years ago called Yvonne.

Sessions with her were not comfortable. My brain physically ached afterwards. I would come out without a single answer and a head full of better questions.

She never once handed me comfort. She handed me growth.

I think about that quite a bit now, because we have built a story in this country that says working it out on your own is the honourable way. That asking is a bit of a wobble. That needing somebody in your corner means you are not quite cut out for it.

Solo is slower. That is all there is to it.

And there are two things people get muddled here, so here they are plainly.

A mentor has walked your trail. They know the loose rocks and the bends where people come off. It is long, it is relational, and it is far more walk with me than let me fix you.

A coach does not hand you the map at all. They ask the question that wakes something up in you, because they reckon you already have the answer and nobody has asked you properly yet.

Stuck on how? You want a mentor. Stuck on what, or on whether? You want a coach.

Most of the people I meet who are grinding away on their own have never worked out which of the two they need, so they end up with neither and call it independence.

Who is in your corner at the minute?

And when did they last tell you something you did not want to hear?
