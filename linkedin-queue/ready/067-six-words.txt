Pick six words that describe how you are at work.

Go on, properly pick them. Reliable. Calm. Straight talking. Whatever turns up.

Now the uncomfortable half. Ask three people you work with to pick six words for you, without showing them yours.

The gap between those two lists is the most useful thing you will read all year, and hardly anybody goes and gets it.

There is a model for this from 1955, the Johari Window, and the only part you need is the quadrant called the blind spot. What other people can see in you that you cannot see in yourself.

Everybody has one. I am no different.

Mine, for years, was that I thought the word for me was reliable. The word doing the rounds was probably closer to available. Same behaviour. Completely different meaning, and only one of those two is a compliment.

I would never have worked that out by reflecting harder on my own. You cannot think your way into your blind spot. That is what makes it blind.

The only road in is somebody else's mouth.

So pick your three. One who works for you, one alongside you, one who has known you for years and does not need anything from you.

Six words each. No chat beforehand, no explaining what you meant, and no defending yourself when it lands.

Then sit with the gap for a day before you do anything with it.

What do you think the people you work with would put on that list?

And are you willing to find out this week?
