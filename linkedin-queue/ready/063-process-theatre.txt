I was told to apply for a job I had already been promised.

The role was being restructured. My old one was going. They sat me down and said I would definitely get the new one, no question, just a formality. Then they sent me the application form.

So I wrote it. I prepped for the interview. I sat in a room and sold myself to people who had made their minds up weeks earlier.

I got it. And it still took something off me that I did not get back for a long while.

Because if the outcome is already decided, what is the interview actually for?

It is not for the candidate. It is for the paperwork. It is so somebody can say the process was followed and point at a scoring sheet if anyone ever asks.

And the thing about process theatre is this: everybody in the room knows. The person being interviewed knows. The panel knows. Nobody says it out loud, so it goes in the file as fair, and the only thing that really gets built is a quiet little lesson that what you say in here does not count for much.

I have watched owners do this with the best intentions. You know who you want to promote. You have known for months. But you advertise it internally because that feels like the proper way to behave, and three other people spend a fortnight preparing for something that was never on offer.

You have not been fair to them. You have made them compete for a decision you had already taken.

So pick one and mean it.

If it is a genuine process, run it properly and be ready to be surprised by the answer.

If it is not, say so. "I am promoting him. Here is why. Here is what I would need to see from you to be in that conversation next time."

That second one is harder to say and much kinder to hear.

Which of the two did you run last time?
