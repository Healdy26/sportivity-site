"I have not failed. I have just found ten thousand ways that will not work."

Edison. Everybody quotes it. Almost nobody runs a business like it.

Here is the test I would put to you, and it is not whether you say you are fine with failure. It is whether your team believes you.

Because they are not listening to what you announce at the team meeting. They are watching what happened to the last person who got something wrong.

Farson and Keyes wrote a piece years back that has stuck with me, and the part that unsettles people is where they argue you should treat success and failure the same. Not celebrate failure. Nobody is throwing a party for a project that lost money. Treat them the same, because the thing you are actually interested in is what got learned, and that sits in both of them.

Most places do the opposite. A win gets a mention in the newsletter. A mistake gets a review, and the review has a tone, and every person in that room can feel the tone.

Do that twice and you have taught your best people something. Have a go at the safe one. Nobody ever got hauled in over the safe one.

So, honestly.

When something went wrong last, did you ask what happened, or did you ask who did it?

The room could tell the difference. They always can.

If you want people taking a risk for you this year, the cheapest thing you can do is go back to something that failed six months ago and say out loud what you learned from it. Your own one, mind. Not theirs.

Nothing gives people permission faster.
