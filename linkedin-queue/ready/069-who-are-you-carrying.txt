The nicest leader in the building is often the one doing the most damage.

Not the shouter. Everybody sees the shouter coming and everybody has worked out how to handle him.

I mean the warm one. Remembers your kids' names, genuinely asks how you are, and has somebody on his team who has been coasting for two years and has never once been told.

He thinks that is kindness. It is not. It is twenty uncomfortable minutes he keeps putting off.

And here is the bit that gets missed. Everyone else can see it.

They can see what gets carried. They can see who gets covered for, and they are not daft, so they draw the obvious conclusion. The standard is not a standard. It is a preference, and it only lands on the people who are too polite to let it slide.

That is how a decent culture goes quietly soft. Not through bad people. Through a kind leader who could not bear to disappoint somebody.

Kind and tough are not opposites. The best leaders I have worked with are both at once. Warm with the person, firm on the work, and clear at all times about which of the two they are talking about.

So when you do have it, keep those two apart.

Describe the work, not the character. Say what was missing and what it cost. Then ask what is getting in the way, and let the quiet sit there until they answer honestly rather than quickly.

None of that attacks anyone. All of it holds the line.

Avoidance with a friendly face on is still avoidance.

So who are you carrying at the minute?

And how long have you known?
