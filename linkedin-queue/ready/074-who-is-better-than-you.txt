The hardest hire I ever made was somebody better than me at the thing.

Not better in general. Better at the one job I had been doing myself for years and quietly reckoned I was decent at.

I will be honest about the first fortnight. It was horrible. He did it faster and cleaner than I ever had, right in front of me, and some small daft part of my head went: well, what am I for then?

That feeling is the signal you got it right. Nobody told me that at the time and I wish they had.

Because most owners hire the other way without ever deciding to. You bring in people you can out-do on every task in the building. It feels safe and it looks fine. Everybody is competent, nothing you say ever gets questioned, and you are quietly the ceiling on the whole thing.

They cannot grow past you. You chose people you can beat.

So here is a test that takes ten seconds and is a bit uncomfortable. Go through your team in your head, name by name.

Who is better than you at their job?

Not keen. Not reliable. Not lovely. Better.

If you cannot name anybody, that is not a recruitment problem. That is a decision you have been making over and over without once saying it out loud.

And I do understand it. Taking on somebody who can beat you at the thing you built the business on feels like giving away the one patch of ground you are sure of.

But the bit I nearly got wrong was what came next. Let him do it his way. Not your way, quicker. His way. You will hate about a third of it and the work will be better anyway, and that third is your ego talking, not the work.

So go on, name by name.

Who is better than you?
