I blocked four hours for proper deep work last week and I was finished by twelve.

Not half finished. Finished. Everything that mattered, done, in two hours.

And do you know what I felt? Not triumph.

Anxiety.

An empty afternoon sat in front of me and my head started up. You should be doing something. You are being lazy. Go and get busy.

That is old wiring and I know exactly where it came from. Years of quietly measuring my worth in hours at the desk instead of in work that actually landed.

So I sat with it and said, no. The work is done. Done is done.

How did two hours swallow a four hour day? AI, used properly rather than poked at.

I talk my thinking out loud, messy and Northern and half formed, and it all gets captured. Then the structuring and the drafting and the donkey work happen while I keep hold of the judgement. Jobs I would have paid somebody else for two years ago, I now have finished before lunch.

But the tool is not really the point of this.

The point is what I nearly did with the afternoon it handed back to me. I nearly filled it. I nearly went looking for something to be busy with, so the day would look like a proper day from the outside.

That is the bit worth watching in yourself. Because if you get faster at the work and then pour every hour you save straight back into looking busy, you have gained nothing at all. You have just hidden it better.

Busyness is a disease. I say it to clients quite a bit and I am clearly still not immune to it myself.

So if tomorrow afternoon came back to you empty, honestly, what would you do with it?
